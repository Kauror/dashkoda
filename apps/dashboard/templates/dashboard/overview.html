{% extends "dashboard/shell.html" %} {% comment %} Ülevaade — the board's landing page. Layout follows direction 1a of the Overview Refresh design: a headline strip, a two-by-two grid of module cards, and the communication channels that have no source yet. Everything it renders comes from `apps.dashboard.overview.build_overview`, which reads PostgreSQL through each module's own selectors. The template chooses layout and nothing else. Three things are deliberate and should stay that way: - a figure with no connected source renders its empty state, never a zero; - the membership chart draws the board report's total against its own paid count — one source, one definition, one reading date, so the gap between the lines is the paid share and nothing else. The koda.ee directory count is a different definition on a different cadence: it leads the headline strip under its own source name and never appears on those axes; - each headline cell carries its own module's counts, and every count states the period it was measured over. There is no separate strip repeating those same movements underneath. The mockup this follows carries synthetic numbers and says so on its own banner. None of them reached this file. {% endcomment %} {% block title %}Ülevaade – DashKoda{% endblock %} {% block content %}

{# -------- Liikmeskond -------- #} {% comment %} The board report's own figures: its member total, its paid count and the fee collection those two are read alongside. Every figure here comes off one monthly report and says so beneath itself. The koda.ee directory total is not repeated here. It is a different definition on a different cadence, it leads the headline strip under its own source name, and stating it twice on one page is what invites a reader to subtract one total from the other. The paid share is computed inside the board report alone — dividing the internal paid count by the public directory total would be a ratio between two different definitions and is never shown. Both trends run on one pair of axes, as a solid and a dashed line. The board reads this card to see the gap between the two, and two separate drawings with two independent scales made that gap a matter of guesswork. Sharing the axes is a drawing decision and nothing more: the lines keep their own labels, their own sources and their own cadences, they are never summed, and neither is ever continued into the other. {% endcomment %}
