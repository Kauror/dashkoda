{% comment %} One domain card. Six of them are the front page's first screen. Context: card – an `ExecutiveDomainCard` Six of these sit in a grid — three columns on a desktop, two on a tablet, one on a phone — so the card is compact by contract rather than by taste. Its shape is fixed and identical on all six, which is what lets a reader scan them: DOMAIN Vaata → BIG NUMBER unit comparison fact label value baseline value fact label value period line The way out moved into the header on 2026-08-18. It was a footer row of its own, which cost every card a rule and a line of height to hold one link — and put six identical `Vaata …` links along the bottom edge of the grid, where they read as a row of buttons rather than as each card's own next step. What this card deliberately does not render: - a **question line** or a **meaning sentence**. Both restated the figure immediately above them; - a **sparkline**. Six cards fit two rows only because none of them is tall, and a trend a couple of centimetres wide cannot be read anyway; - a **source caption under every number**. Provenance lives in the data — `ExecutiveMetric` and `ExecutiveFact` still carry `source` and `as_of`, and `Andmete seis` at `/haldus/` is built from the same objects; - a **status colour for the card as a whole**. There is no red/amber/green verdict on a domain. What needs attention is in `Tähelepanu` below, decided by the domain that measured it. Direction is never colour alone: the comparison text carries its own sign. {% endcomment %}
