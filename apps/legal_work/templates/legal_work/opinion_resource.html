{% extends "dashboard/shell.html" %} {% comment %} The internal resource page a sent legal topic links to. What it shows is the Chamber's own correspondence about one legal matter: the letter that answered it, and whatever went with that letter. What it must never show is how the match was arrived at — no score, runner-up, margin, evidence code, matcher version or decision label — because a reader is being told "this is the opinion we sent", not "the matcher was 73% confident". The same applies to storage: no filename beyond the sanitised display name, no path, no digest and no database id. The address is the matter's opaque UUID and is stable across legal snapshots. When the matter has left the newest workbook the page still renders, from the durable metadata, with an explicit historical note — a published address that starts returning 404 is worse than one that says the matter is no longer in the current workbook. Styling is the shared `dk-*` vocabulary and the semantic colour tokens, like every other page. It was built with raw `slate-*` and `amber-*` utilities, which made this the one page whose surfaces, borders and warning colour were pinned to literal palette values instead of following the theme. The markup, the layout and the information hierarchy are unchanged. {% endcomment %} {% block title %}Arvamus – DashKoda{% endblock %} {% block content %}
